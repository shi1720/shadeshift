ShadeShift | A funded shade plan for major events | Rice Urban Sustainability Hackathon

# YouTube description

The ticket gets you a seat. It does not get you shade.

ShadeShift helps event operators allocate a limited budget across shade, queue operations and refill access. This demo shows a Houston scenario with a $75,000 cap. Both the starting and optimized portfolios spend $70,000. The optimized plan reduces modeled direct-sun exposure by 31.25%, with visible tradeoffs in refill capacity and community-weighted investment.

The optimizer constrains initial deployment spend. At 12 event windows per year, the starting plan estimates $171,200 in first-year program cost and the optimized plan $251,400, including assumed repeat queue staffing, maintenance and refill operation allowances. Equal initial spend does not mean equal annual cost.

Live application: https://shadeshift-city.web.app
Source code: https://github.com/shi1720/shadeshift

Built for Track 3 of the Rice University Urban Sustainability Hackathon, using FIFA World Cup 2026 as a case for recurring sports, concerts and festivals.

Evidence includes 654 historical Houston air-temperature observations, official transit anchors and consistent historical weather benchmarks for all 11 U.S. host locations. Visitor counts, dwell time, shade, cost and intervention effects are editable planning assumptions. These results are not measured deployment outcomes, clinical risk estimates or forecasts.

Stack: Vite, React, TypeScript, Leaflet, Recharts, Firebase Hosting, Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore.

Pricing hypothesis: $5,000 for an assisted two-event pilot, then $6,000 annually per venue. Physical equipment and installation are separate. No customer commitment is implied.

Data attribution: H3AT, CAPA Strategies, Houston Advanced Research Center, Harris County METRO, OpenStreetMap contributors, Open-Meteo and ECMWF ERA5/Copernicus. This independent project does not imply endorsement by FIFA, Rice or the data providers.
